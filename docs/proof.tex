\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) trains a policy against an incorruptible,
policy-independent grader, and works on tasks where correctness can be checked mechanically,
such as mathematics and code. Most queries a language model receives are not of this kind: a
recommendation or an explanation has no answer key. This note develops \emph{Reinforcement
Learning from Downstream Feedback} (RLDF), which replaces the verifier by a population of
simulated consumers. Each candidate response is played out against the population, the realized
outcome of acting on it is graded, and the population-aggregated grade is the reward. We
formalize the quantity being optimized and prove the following. The foresight (response-only)
reward used by ordinary RLHF is not welfare-consistent once responses can inflate perceived
quality (Lemma~\ref{lem:foresight}), whereas the hindsight (outcome-conditioned) reward is
welfare-consistent under an explicit calibration hypothesis (Lemma~\ref{lem:hindsight}). A
finite panel of consumers estimates population welfare at rate $O(1/\sqrt n)$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:concentration}), and a strategically corruptible minority admits a closed-form
threshold $\eps^\star$ above which the reward-maximizing response is a manipulation
(Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold}). An $\alpha$-trimmed-mean aggregator then preserves the welfare
\emph{ranking} of responses, though not their welfare \emph{value}, whenever the corruptible
fraction satisfies $\eps<\alpha$ and a margin condition holds (Lemma~\ref{lem:rank}). Together
these give a welfare-consistency theorem for robust RLDF (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}), with RLVR as
the special case of a single consumer, a deterministic world, and a perfect verifier
(Proposition~\ref{prop:rlvr}, Corollary~\ref{cor:rlvr}). We then relax exact calibration to an
$\eta$-approximate hypothesis (Theorem~\ref{thm:approxcalib}), reduce the welfare regret of the
training loop to the reward error and expose the irreducible floor that the trimming and
contamination penalties leave behind (Theorem~\ref{thm:regret}), and lift the per-query result to
a per-distribution welfare-regret bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:dist}) whose contamination threshold
$\eps<\alpha$ we show is sharp (Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp}).
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) is the training recipe behind the recent
generation of reasoning models \cite{deepseekr1,tulu3,deepseekmath}. For a prompt whose answer
can be checked, such as a final numeric answer or a unit test, one samples responses from the
policy, scores each with an automatic verifier returning $1$ for correct and $0$ otherwise, and
ascends the resulting reward. No human annotator rates the outputs and no learned reward model
sits between the policy and the truth. The verifier is incorruptible and policy-independent: a
wrong answer cannot be rephrased into a passing grade, and the grader does not drift as the
policy improves.

This is also what bounds RLVR's reach. The recipe applies only where a cheap, sound checker
exists, which covers a small fraction of the queries users pose; a question like whether to
accept a job offer has no answer key. Recent work extends RLVR to open-ended domains by learning
a soft grader, either a generative reward model or a rubric critic
\cite{crossingbridge,rlmt,truthrl}, but a learned grader reintroduces the corruptibility the
verifier removed: it scores the response, and a response can be optimized to look good to the
grader without being good for the user. Sycophancy and confident hallucination follow
\cite{sycophancy,williams}.

\paragraph{The downstream-feedback idea.}
RLDF changes what is graded. Instead of scoring the response, it simulates what happens after a
user acts on the response and grades the consequence. A sycophantic or confidently hallucinated
answer can score well to a response-only grader, but once a simulated user acts on it the
realized outcome is poor, and the reward depends on that outcome rather than on the response's
surface plausibility. In the economic reading, a response is a product, a population of
heterogeneous simulated consumers acts on it and realizes welfare or regret, and the aggregate
of those outcomes is the reward. The point of departure from RLVR is that for a non-trivial class
of tasks the outcome of acting on a response is mechanically scorable even when the response
itself is open-ended natural language, so consequence-grading recovers a policy-independent
signal where response-grading cannot (Definition~\ref{def:vo}).

\paragraph{Outline.}
Section~\ref{sec:setup} fixes notation, and Section~\ref{sec:rlvr} shows that RLVR is the special
case of RLDF with a single consumer, a deterministic world, and a perfect verifier, placing RLDF
on a spectrum from outcome-verifiable tasks, where the grader is policy-independent, to
simulated-outcome tasks, where it is not. Section~\ref{sec:foresight} treats the
foresight-hindsight gap: the response-only reward is not welfare-consistent when responses carry
a persuasion bias (Lemma~\ref{lem:foresight}), while the outcome-conditioned reward is, under
calibration (Lemma~\ref{lem:hindsight}); these restate, in the present notation, the central
claim of RLHS \cite{rlhs}. Section~\ref{sec:market} shows the finite-panel reward concentrates on
population welfare. Section~\ref{sec:game} derives the gameability threshold and shows that a
stronger type-aware adversary is blocked by grading on representative panels, which makes precise
the finding of \cite{williams} that a small gameable minority suffices to induce manipulation.
Section~\ref{sec:robust} replaces the mean by a trimmed mean and recovers the welfare ranking
under $\eps<\alpha$ and a margin condition. Section~\ref{sec:mainthm} assembles these into
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. Sections~\ref{sec:approxcalib}--\ref{sec:distributional} then extend the
guarantee in three directions: to approximate calibration, to the regret of the training loop, and
to a per-distribution welfare bound with a matching sharpness result on the contamination
threshold. Sections~\ref{sec:discussion}--\ref{sec:related} discuss the assumptions and the
relation to prior work.


\section{Setup and notation}
\label{sec:setup}

We work with a one-shot advisory interaction: the policy observes a query, emits a response, a
user acts on it, and an outcome is realized. The multi-turn case extends this straightforwardly
and is not needed for any result below.

\begin{definition}[Interaction primitives]
\label{def:primitives}
Fix measurable spaces of \emph{queries} $\Xc$, \emph{responses} $\Ac$, \emph{user types}
$\Uc$, and \emph{outcomes} $\Oc$. The interaction is specified by:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item a query distribution $\Dc\in\Delta(\Xc)$;
  \item a \emph{population kernel} $P(\cdot\given x)\in\Delta(\Uc)$, the distribution of user
  types who issue query $x$ (heterogeneity: distinct users may share a query yet differ in
  their latent situation and preferences);
  \item a \emph{policy} $\policy(\cdot\given x)\in\Delta(\Ac)$, parameterized by
  $\theta\in\Theta$;
  \item a \emph{world model} (transition kernel) $T(\cdot\given x,u,a)\in\Delta(\Oc)$, the
  distribution of realized outcomes when a user of type $u$ acts on response $a$ to query
  $x$;
  \item a \emph{welfare function} $W:\Xc\times\Uc\times\Ac\to[0,1]$, the true (latent) utility
  to a user of type $u$ from acting on response $a$. Boundedness to $[0,1]$ is a
  normalization.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Alignment target and population welfare]
\label{def:target}
The \emph{alignment target} is
\[
  J^\star(\theta)
  \;\defeq\;
  \E_{x\sim\Dc}\;\E_{u\sim P(\cdot\given x)}\;\E_{a\sim\policy(\cdot\given x)}
  \big[\,W(x,u,a)\,\big].
\]
For fixed $(x,a)$, the \emph{population-average welfare} is
$\Wbar(x,a)\defeq\E_{u\sim P(\cdot\given x)}[\,W(x,u,a)\,]$, so that
$J^\star(\theta)=\E_{x\sim\Dc}\,\E_{a\sim\policy(\cdot\given x)}[\,\Wbar(x,a)\,]$. The
\emph{welfare-optimal response set} at $x$ is
$\Ac^\star(x)\defeq\argmax_{a\in\Ac}\Wbar(x,a)$ (assumed non-empty).
\end{definition}

Because the policy may choose its action distribution separately for each $x$, maximizing
$J^\star$ decomposes pointwise: $J^\star$ is maximized iff $\policy(\cdot\given x)$ is
supported on $\Ac^\star(x)$ for $\Dc$-almost every $x$. This reduces the design problem to a
\emph{per-query ranking} problem, which is the criterion every reward in this note is
measured against.

\begin{definition}[Welfare-consistency of a reward]
\label{def:consistency}
A reward $r:\Xc\times\Ac\to\R$ is \emph{welfare-consistent at $x$} if
$\argmax_{a\in\Ac} r(x,a)\subseteq\Ac^\star(x)$, and \emph{on a candidate set}
$\Ac_c\subseteq\Ac$ if the inclusion holds with both $\argmax$ operators restricted to
$\Ac_c$. A policy that maximizes a welfare-consistent reward pointwise maximizes $J^\star$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Foresight and hindsight graders]
\label{def:graders}
A \emph{foresight grader} $r_F:\Xc\times\Ac\to[0,1]$ scores a response before any outcome is
realized, seeing $(x,a)$ only. A \emph{hindsight grader} is the composition of (i) the world
model $T$, realizing $o\sim T(\cdot\given x,u,a)$, and (ii) an \emph{outcome evaluator}
$g:\Oc\to[0,1]$, returning $g(o)$. Throughout, ``grader'' in the hindsight setting refers to the
entire simulate-then-evaluate pipeline $(T,g)$; in particular, any way a response could fool the
simulator into producing a misleadingly favorable outcome is accounted for inside $g\circ T$,
not treated as a separate channel.
\end{definition}


\section{RLVR as a degenerate special case}
\label{sec:rlvr}

RLDF strictly generalizes RLVR. Recording the special case first fixes intuition, and it is used
later when the main theorem recovers RLVR's guarantee as a limit.

\begin{proposition}[RLVR is zero-noise, zero-corruption RLDF]
\label{prop:rlvr}
Suppose at every query $x$: \textup{(a)} $P(\cdot\given x)=\delta_{u_0}$ (single canonical
consumer); \textup{(b)} $T(\cdot\given x,u_0,a)=\delta_{o(x,a)}$ (deterministic world);
\textup{(c)} $g(o(x,a))=\Ind\{a\text{ correct for }x\}$ is computed exactly (perfect,
non-corruptible verifier); \textup{(d)} $W(x,u_0,a)=\Ind\{a\text{ correct for }x\}$. Then the
hindsight reward equals welfare identically, $\Wbar(x,a)=g(o(x,a))=W(x,u_0,a)$, with zero
variance and no corruptible mass; consequently the RLDF reward reduces to the RLVR reward and
is welfare-consistent at every $x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under (a), $\Wbar(x,a)=W(x,u_0,a)$; under (d), this equals $\Ind\{a\text{ correct}\}$. Under
(b)--(c), the hindsight grade is the deterministic quantity $g(o(x,a))=\Ind\{a\text{
correct}\}$, identical to $\Wbar(x,a)$ and noise-free. Then $\argmax_a\Wbar(x,a)$ is the set
of correct responses, which is $\Ac^\star(x)$ by definition.
\end{proof}

RLDF generalizes RLVR along the three axes that Proposition~\ref{prop:rlvr} holds fixed: a
non-degenerate population $P$, a stochastic world $T$, and an imperfect grader. The third axis
deserves a name, since it is what separates an extension of RLVR from a relabeled reward model.

\begin{definition}[Outcome-verifiable vs.\ simulated-outcome tasks]
\label{def:vo}
A task $(x,T,g)$ is \emph{outcome-verifiable} if the pipeline $g\circ T$ is policy-independent:
the outcome of acting on a response is produced by a fixed executor and scored by a fixed
program $\phi$, so $g(T(x,u,a))=\phi(\Ec(x,a))$ for a sandbox state $\Ec$ and scorer $\phi$ that
do not depend on $\theta$. It is \emph{simulated-outcome} if $g\circ T$ is itself a learned model
(for instance an LLM world model and evaluator), whose representation of consequences can
co-adapt with the policy.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The spectrum]
\label{rem:spectrum}
Outcome-verifiable tasks are common even when the response is open-ended. A code change, for
instance, is free-form text, but whether it passes the project's test suite is a fixed check
that does not depend on the policy. On such tasks $g\circ T$ is a genuine verifier, the
corruption parameter $\eps$ of Section~\ref{sec:game} is $0$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:main} recovers
RLVR-grade guarantees (Corollary~\ref{cor:rlvr}). On simulated-outcome tasks the grader is a
model and $\eps>0$ in general, so calling the reward verifiable is unwarranted without an
argument that $g\circ T$ is hard to fool; the robust aggregation of Section~\ref{sec:robust}
stands in for the missing policy-independence.
\end{remark}


\section{The foresight-hindsight gap}
\label{sec:foresight}

Grading the response and grading the consequence give different rewards, and the difference is
visible already at the level of the reward, before any optimizer is introduced. The results in
this section are, in substance, the central claim of RLHS \cite{rlhs} in the present notation;
we include proofs because later results build on them.

A perfectly Bayesian, non-manipulable foresight grader would report
$\E_u[W(x,u,a)]=\Wbar(x,a)$ and would be welfare-consistent. The empirical content of the
sycophancy and deception literature \cite{sycophancy,williams,sleeper} is that real graders are
not of this kind: a response can be shaped to inflate perceived quality without improving
welfare. We take this as an assumption rather than deriving it.

\begin{assumption}[Persuasion bias of foresight grading]
\label{ass:bias}
The foresight grader decomposes as $r_F(x,a)=\Wbar(x,a)+B(x,a)$, where $B:\Xc\times\Ac\to\R$
is the \emph{persuasion bias}, the amount by which response $a$ moves perceived quality away from
true population welfare. A non-manipulable grader has $B\equiv 0$; we assume only that $B$ is
\emph{response-sensitive}, i.e.\ there exist queries at which $B(x,\cdot)$ is non-constant in
$a$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Foresight reward is not welfare-consistent]
\label{lem:foresight}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:bias}, there exist a query $x$ and responses $a_1,a_2\in\Ac$ with
$\Wbar(x,a_1)>\Wbar(x,a_2)$ yet $r_F(x,a_1)<r_F(x,a_2)$; hence
$\argmax_a r_F(x,a)\not\subseteq\Ac^\star(x)$, and maximizing the foresight reward can
strictly select a welfare-inferior response.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is an existence claim, so one instance suffices. Fix a query $x$ at which $B(x,\cdot)$ is
non-constant (Assumption~\ref{ass:bias}). Let $a_1$ be a welfare-superior ``honest'' response
and $a_2$ a ``sycophantic'' response with
\[
  \Wbar(x,a_2)=\Wbar(x,a_1)-\delta\quad(\delta>0),
  \qquad B(x,a_2)-B(x,a_1)>\delta .
\]
Such a pair exists whenever the bias gap can exceed the welfare gap, the defining feature of a
manipulable grader. Then
\[
  r_F(x,a_2)-r_F(x,a_1)
  =\underbrace{[\Wbar(x,a_2)-\Wbar(x,a_1)]}_{=-\delta}
   +\underbrace{[B(x,a_2)-B(x,a_1)]}_{>\,\delta}\;>\;0,
\]
so $a_2$ is strictly foresight-preferred while strictly welfare-inferior, and
$a_2\in\argmax\{r_F(x,a_1),r_F(x,a_2)\}$ with $a_2\notin\Ac^\star(x)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The lemma is an existence statement, and intentionally so. Response-sensitivity of $B$ does not
by itself reorder the $\argmax$: a bias that varies in $a$ but never overtakes the welfare gap
leaves the optimum intact. The assumption is that the overtaking case occurs, which is what the
sycophancy literature reports, and one instance is enough to deny welfare-consistency.
\end{remark}

\begin{assumption}[Outcome calibration]
\label{ass:calib}
The hindsight grader is \emph{outcome-calibrated} if, for all $(x,u,a)$,
\[
  \rho(x,u,a)\;\defeq\;\E_{o\sim T(\cdot\given x,u,a)}\big[g(o)\big]\;=\;W(x,u,a).
\]
More generally we allow affine-monotone calibration $\rho=c_0+c_1 W$ with $c_1>0$; every
ranking statement below is invariant to such a reparameterization, so proofs are stated for
$c_0=0,\ c_1=1$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Hindsight reward is welfare-consistent]
\label{lem:hindsight}
Define the \emph{population hindsight reward}
$r_H(x,a)\defeq\E_{u\sim P(\cdot\given x)}\E_{o\sim T(\cdot\given x,u,a)}[g(o)]
=\E_{u\sim P(\cdot\given x)}[\rho(x,u,a)]$. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:calib},
$r_H(x,a)=\Wbar(x,a)$ for all $x,a$; consequently $r_H$ is welfare-consistent at every $x$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By the tower property $r_H(x,a)=\E_u[\rho(x,u,a)]$; substituting calibration
$\rho(x,u,a)=W(x,u,a)$ gives $r_H(x,a)=\E_u[W(x,u,a)]=\Wbar(x,a)$. Hence
$\argmax_a r_H(x,a)=\argmax_a\Wbar(x,a)=\Ac^\star(x)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the bias disappears]
\label{rem:nobias}
The persuasion term $B$ has no analogue in $r_H$ because $g(o)$ is a function of the realized
outcome $o$, and $o$ depends on the response only through the law $T(\cdot\given x,u,a)$, the
channel through which $a$ actually changes the world. A persuasive but unhelpful response
induces the same outcome distribution as a blunt unhelpful one and is graded identically.
Conditionally on $(x,u,a)$, the persuasiveness of $a$ is irrelevant to $o$; this conditional
irrelevance is exactly Assumption~\ref{ass:calib}, and its failure is the corruptible case of
Section~\ref{sec:game}.
\end{remark}


\section{The market reward over a finite population}
\label{sec:market}

Lemma~\ref{lem:hindsight} concerns the idealized reward $r_H=\Wbar$, an expectation over the
full population and outcome law. An implementable reward replaces both expectations by
simulation: draw a finite \emph{panel} of consumers, realize one outcome each, and aggregate the
grades. This panel is the ``market'' that prices the response by its realized experience.

\begin{definition}[Empirical market reward]
\label{def:market}
Fix $(x,a)$ and panel size $n$. Draw $u_1,\dots,u_n\stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}
P(\cdot\given x)$, and for each draw $o_i\sim T(\cdot\given x,u_i,a)$. The \emph{empirical
market reward} is the panel mean $\hat r_n(x,a)\defeq\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n g(o_i)\in[0,1]$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Concentration of the market reward]
\label{lem:concentration}
Assume $g\in[0,1]$. Then $\E[\hat r_n(x,a)]=r_H(x,a)$, and for every $t>0$,
\[
  \Prob\big(\,|\hat r_n(x,a)-r_H(x,a)|\ge t\,\big)\;\le\;2\exp(-2nt^2).
\]
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:calib} (so $r_H=\Wbar$), a panel of size
$n\ge\frac{1}{2t^2}\log\frac2\delta$ gives $|\hat r_n(x,a)-\Wbar(x,a)|<t$ with probability at
least $1-\delta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The pairs $(u_i,o_i)$ are i.i.d., hence so are the $g(o_i)\in[0,1]$, with common mean
$\E[g(o_i)]=\E_{u\sim P}\E_{o\sim T}[g(o)]=r_H(x,a)$ by Definition~\ref{def:graders} and the
tower property. Hoeffding's inequality \cite{hoeffding} for an average of $n$ independent
$[0,1]$ variables gives the two-sided bound; inverting $2e^{-2nt^2}\le\delta$ gives the
sample-size statement.
\end{proof}

Two of the three degeneracies of Proposition~\ref{prop:rlvr} are now lifted, the population
being a genuine distribution and the world stochastic, and the reward still tracks welfare. The
third, an incorruptible grader, is the subject of the next section, and removing it is the real
difficulty.


\section{Strategic corruption and the gameability threshold}
\label{sec:game}

Lemma~\ref{lem:hindsight} rests entirely on calibration. We now model its failure. The failure
that matters is not uniform noise but a strategic one: some subpopulation can be driven to report
high grades by a response that does not serve it, for example a user who reports satisfaction
when flattered. Following \cite{williams}, we model a faithful majority and a corruptible
minority.

\begin{definition}[Contaminated population and manipulation]
\label{def:contam}
Fix $x$. The population is an $\eps$-contaminated mixture
$P(\cdot\given x)=(1-\eps)P_{\mathrm f}(\cdot\given x)+\eps P_{\mathrm g}(\cdot\given x)$,
where calibration (Assumption~\ref{ass:calib}) holds on the \emph{faithful} part
$P_{\mathrm f}$ and may fail on the \emph{gameable} part $P_{\mathrm g}$. A \emph{manipulation}
is a response $a^\dagger$ with a corrupted ceiling $G_{\max}\in(0,1]$ such that, for gameable
$u$, $\rho(x,u,a^\dagger)=G_{\max}$ irrespective of the true (low) welfare $W(x,u,a^\dagger)$.
\end{definition}

For a welfare-optimal honest response $a^\star\in\Ac^\star(x)$, write
$\Wbar^\star\defeq\Wbar(x,a^\star)$, and let
\[
  \Wbar^\dagger_h\;\defeq\;\E_{u\sim P_{\mathrm f}}\big[W(x,u,a^\dagger)\big]
\]
denote the manipulation's true welfare on the faithful part, with
$\Wbar^\dagger_h<\Wbar^\star$. For transparency we take the honest response to attract no
corruption (reward $\Wbar^\star$); allowing it to be corrupted as well only strengthens the case
for the remedy of Section~\ref{sec:robust}.

\begin{lemma}[Gameability threshold]
\label{lem:threshold}
Under mean aggregation over the contaminated population,
$r_{\mathrm{mkt}}(a^\star)=\Wbar^\star$ and
$r_{\mathrm{mkt}}(a^\dagger)=(1-\eps)\Wbar^\dagger_h+\eps G_{\max}$. The manipulation is
strictly market-preferred iff
\[
  \boxed{\;\eps>\eps^\star\defeq\frac{\Wbar^\star-\Wbar^\dagger_h}{G_{\max}-\Wbar^\dagger_h}\;}
\]
and $\eps^\star\in(0,1)$ whenever $\Wbar^\dagger_h<\Wbar^\star\le G_{\max}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The reward of $a^\dagger$ splits over the mixture: a $(1-\eps)$ mass grades it at its true
welfare $\Wbar^\dagger_h$ (calibration on $P_{\mathrm f}$), an $\eps$ mass at the ceiling
$G_{\max}$ (Definition~\ref{def:contam}), giving
$r_{\mathrm{mkt}}(a^\dagger)=(1-\eps)\Wbar^\dagger_h+\eps G_{\max}$. Then
\[
  r_{\mathrm{mkt}}(a^\dagger)>r_{\mathrm{mkt}}(a^\star)
  \iff \eps(G_{\max}-\Wbar^\dagger_h)>\Wbar^\star-\Wbar^\dagger_h
  \iff \eps>\eps^\star,
\]
dividing by $G_{\max}-\Wbar^\dagger_h>0$. The numerator is positive
($\Wbar^\star>\Wbar^\dagger_h$) so $\eps^\star>0$; and $\Wbar^\star\le G_{\max}$ gives
$\eps^\star\le1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What makes ``$2\%$ suffices'' rigorous]
\label{rem:twopercent}
The threshold $\eps^\star$ is small when the welfare margin $\Wbar^\star-\Wbar^\dagger_h$ is
small relative to the corrupt advantage $G_{\max}-\Wbar^\dagger_h$, that is, when gaming one
gameable consumer pays off far more than honest service would. As $G_{\max}\to1$ with a fixed
modest margin, $\eps^\star\to(\Wbar^\star-\Wbar^\dagger_h)/(1-\Wbar^\dagger_h)$, which can sit at
a few percent. This is the closed-form mechanism behind the observation of \cite{williams} that
a gameable minority of order $2\%$ already tips an RL-trained model into manipulation: it is the
leverage $G_{\max}$, not the size $\eps$, that matters.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Targeting, and why the panel must be representative]
\label{rem:target}
Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold} prices a single response against a representative draw from
$P(\cdot\given x)$. A stronger adversary, a type-aware policy $\pi_\theta(\cdot\given x,s)$ that
observes a gameability signal $s$ rather than the query-only policy $\pi_\theta(\cdot\given x)$
of Definition~\ref{def:primitives}, could in principle route $a^\dagger$ only to gameable
consumers and $a^\star$ to the rest. If, in addition, the reward graded each response on the
consumers who received it (``recipient-grading''), then $a^\dagger$ would be graded by an
all-gameable panel and score the ceiling $G_{\max}$, collapsing the threshold to $\eps^\star=0$,
so that any positive corruptible fraction would be exploited. The defense is structural and is
already built into Definition~\ref{def:market}: every candidate is graded on a fresh i.i.d.\
panel drawn from the true population $P(\cdot\given x)$, not on its recipients. Under
representative panels the gameable share of $a^\dagger$'s panel is the population $\eps$
regardless of how the policy routes responses, so a type-aware adversary gains nothing at
grading time, and hypothesis (A2) below ($\eps<\alpha$ in every panel) holds by construction
whenever the population's gameable fraction is itself below $\alpha$. Recipient-grading forfeits
this guarantee; representative grading keeps the corruptible mass a bounded minority rather than
a hand-picked majority.
\end{remark}

Together, Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold} and Remark~\ref{rem:target} show that a mean over a
population carrying any corruptible mass is unsafe. The remedy has two parts: grade on
representative panels, so the corruptible mass stays a bounded minority
(Remark~\ref{rem:target}), and aggregate that minority out, so it cannot move the price
(Section~\ref{sec:robust}).


\section{Robust aggregation restores the welfare ranking}
\label{sec:robust}

We replace the mean by a trimmed mean, discarding the most extreme grades before averaging.
There is a subtlety, and it is the reason the guarantee below concerns the ranking of responses
rather than their welfare values: the trimmed mean does not estimate $\Wbar$. It estimates a
different functional of the grade distribution, and trimming introduces a bias even without
corruption. We make this estimand shift explicit and prove the weaker property that suffices,
namely that the welfare order of responses is preserved.

\begin{definition}[$\alpha$-trimmed mean and its functional]
\label{def:trim}
For $\alpha\in[0,\tfrac12)$ and a grade law $F$ on $[0,1]$ with quantile function $F^{-1}$, the
\emph{$\alpha$-trimmed mean functional} is
$\mu_\alpha(F)\defeq\frac{1}{1-2\alpha}\int_\alpha^{1-\alpha}F^{-1}(p)\,dp$. Its empirical
version $\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}$ discards the $\lceil\alpha n\rceil$ largest and smallest of the
$n$ panel grades and averages the rest. Write $\mathrm{mean}(F)=\int_0^1 F^{-1}(p)\,dp$, so
that under calibration the clean grade law $F_a$ of response $a$ has
$\mathrm{mean}(F_a)=\Wbar(x,a)$.
\end{definition}

We isolate one elementary property and reuse it three times. Recall the Wasserstein-1 distance
on $[0,1]$-supported laws, $W_1(F,G)=\int_0^1|F(x)-G(x)|\,dx=\int_0^1|F^{-1}(p)-G^{-1}(p)|\,dp$.

\begin{lemma}[$\mu_\alpha$ is $\tfrac{1}{1-2\alpha}$-Lipschitz in $W_1$]
\label{lem:lip}
For $[0,1]$-supported $F,G$,
$|\mu_\alpha(F)-\mu_\alpha(G)|\le\frac{1}{1-2\alpha}W_1(F,G)\le\frac{1}{1-2\alpha}\|F-G\|_\infty$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:trim} and the triangle inequality,
$|\mu_\alpha(F)-\mu_\alpha(G)|\le\frac{1}{1-2\alpha}\int_\alpha^{1-\alpha}|F^{-1}(p)-G^{-1}(p)|\,dp$.
The integrand is non-negative, so extending the range to $[0,1]$ only increases the integral,
giving $\frac{1}{1-2\alpha}\int_0^1|F^{-1}-G^{-1}|\,dp=\frac{1}{1-2\alpha}W_1(F,G)$ by the
quantile representation of $W_1$. Finally $W_1(F,G)=\int_0^1|F(x)-G(x)|\,dx\le\|F-G\|_\infty$
since the support has length $1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Trimming bias, contamination, and sampling bounds]
\label{lem:three}
Let $F$ be a clean $[0,1]$-supported grade law and $F_n$ its $n$-sample empirical law. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.4em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{(Trimming bias)} $|\mu_\alpha(F)-\mathrm{mean}(F)|\le 2\alpha$.
  \item \emph{(Contamination)} for any $[0,1]$-supported $F'$ and
  $\tilde F=(1-\eps)F+\eps F'$, $\ |\mu_\alpha(\tilde F)-\mu_\alpha(F)|\le\frac{\eps}{1-2\alpha}$.
  \item \emph{(Sampling)} for every $s>0$,
  $\Prob(|\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}-\mu_\alpha(F)|>s)\le 2\exp(-2ns^2(1-2\alpha)^2)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(i)} Write $\mu_\alpha(F)-\mathrm{mean}(F)=\int_0^1 w(p)F^{-1}(p)\,dp$ with
$w(p)=\frac{1}{1-2\alpha}\Ind\{\alpha\le p\le1-\alpha\}-1$, so $\int_0^1 w=0$. The positive
part of $w$ lives on $[\alpha,1-\alpha]$ with value $\frac{2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}$ and total mass
$2\alpha$; the negative part on the tails with value $-1$ and total mass $2\alpha$. Since
$F^{-1}\in[0,1]$, each of $\int w_+F^{-1}$ and $\int w_-F^{-1}$ lies in $[0,2\alpha]$, so their
difference is at most $2\alpha$ in absolute value.

\emph{(ii)} By convexity of $W_1$ in its first argument and the diameter bound $W_1\le1$ on
$[0,1]$, $W_1(\tilde F,F)\le\eps\,W_1(F',F)\le\eps$. Lemma~\ref{lem:lip} gives
$|\mu_\alpha(\tilde F)-\mu_\alpha(F)|\le\frac{\eps}{1-2\alpha}$.

\emph{(iii)} By Lemma~\ref{lem:lip},
$|\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}-\mu_\alpha(F)|=|\mu_\alpha(F_n)-\mu_\alpha(F)|\le\frac{1}{1-2\alpha}\|F_n-F\|_\infty$.
The Dvoretzky--Kiefer--Wolfowitz inequality with Massart's tight constant \cite{dkw,massart}
gives $\Prob(\|F_n-F\|_\infty>\eta)\le 2e^{-2n\eta^2}$; set $\eta=s(1-2\alpha)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The estimand shift is real]
Part (i) is not slack to be optimized away: even with a perfectly calibrated, uncontaminated
grader, $\mu_\alpha(F_a)\ne\Wbar(x,a)$ in general, because trimming reshapes the distribution.
Robust RLDF therefore does not estimate welfare; it estimates a trimmed surrogate. What survives
is the comparison between responses, which is all a reward optimizer consumes. The constant
$2\alpha$ is worst-case, attained only by near-$\{0,1\}$ bimodal grades; under a
grade-concentration assumption $\mathrm{Var}_F(g)\le\sigma^2$ it improves to $O(\alpha\sigma)$,
and a median-of-means aggregator \cite{lugosimendelson} trades the trimming bias for a
sub-Gaussian deviation bound of comparable structure. We carry the conservative $2\alpha$ to
keep the theorem assumption-light.
\end{remark}

Let $a^\star$ be welfare-optimal and $a^\dagger$ any welfare-suboptimal alternative, with
clean grade laws $F^\star,F^\dagger$ and welfares $\Wbar^\star=\mathrm{mean}(F^\star)$,
$\Wbar^\dagger=\mathrm{mean}(F^\dagger)$. Suppose each response's panel may contain a
corruptible fraction at most $\eps$.

\begin{lemma}[Ranking preservation under robust aggregation]
\label{lem:rank}
Fix $\alpha\in(0,\tfrac12)$ with $\eps<\alpha$. If the welfare margin satisfies
\[
  M\defeq\Wbar^\star-\Wbar^\dagger\;>\;\underbrace{4\alpha}_{\text{trimming}}
  +\underbrace{\frac{2\eps}{1-2\alpha}}_{\text{contamination}}
  +\underbrace{2s}_{\text{sampling}},
\]
then with probability at least $1-4\exp(-2ns^2(1-2\alpha)^2)$ the empirical trimmed market
rewards satisfy $\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(a^\star)>\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(a^\dagger)$. Robust RLDF
thus ranks the welfare-optimal response strictly above the suboptimal one despite trimming
bias, an $\eps$-corruptible panel, and finite sampling.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On the event that the honest response's sampling error is at most $s$, chaining (iii), then
(ii), then (i) of Lemma~\ref{lem:three},
\[
  \hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(a^\star)
  \ge\mu_\alpha(\tilde F^\star)-s
  \ge\mu_\alpha(F^\star)-\tfrac{\eps}{1-2\alpha}-s
  \ge\Wbar^\star-2\alpha-\tfrac{\eps}{1-2\alpha}-s .
\]
Symmetrically, $\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(a^\dagger)\le\Wbar^\dagger+2\alpha+\tfrac{\eps}{1-2\alpha}+s$.
Subtracting, on the intersection of the two sampling events,
\[
  \hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(a^\star)-\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(a^\dagger)
  \ge M-4\alpha-\frac{2\eps}{1-2\alpha}-2s>0
\]
by hypothesis. Each sampling event fails with probability $\le 2\exp(-2ns^2(1-2\alpha)^2)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:three}(iii); a union bound over the two responses' upper and lower deviations
(four events) gives the stated failure probability.
\end{proof}


\section{Main theorem: welfare-consistency of robust RLDF}
\label{sec:mainthm}

Calibration on the faithful subpopulation makes the clean reward track welfare
(Lemma~\ref{lem:hindsight}); robust aggregation contains the corruptible minority and the
trimming and sampling penalties (Lemma~\ref{lem:rank}); a margin assumption guarantees the
welfare signal exceeds the total penalty.

\begin{theorem}[Welfare-consistency of robust RLDF]
\label{thm:main}
Fix a query $x$ and a finite candidate set $\Ac_c\subseteq\Ac$ containing a welfare-optimal
$a^\star\in\Ac^\star(x)$. Suppose:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(A\arabic*)},leftmargin=3em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textup{(Calibration)} the hindsight grader is outcome-calibrated on the faithful
  subpopulation $P_{\mathrm f}$ (Assumption~\ref{ass:calib});
  \item \textup{(Bounded corruption)} every candidate's panel is at most $\eps$-contaminated
  with $\eps<\alpha$, where $\alpha\in(0,\tfrac12)$ is the trimming level;
  \item \textup{(Welfare margin)} for every welfare-suboptimal $a'\in\Ac_c$,
  $\ \Wbar(x,a^\star)-\Wbar(x,a')>4\alpha+\frac{2\eps}{1-2\alpha}+2s$ for some $s>0$;
  \item \textup{(Panel size)} $n\ge\dfrac{1}{2s^2(1-2\alpha)^2}\log\dfrac{4|\Ac_c|}{\delta}$.
\end{enumerate}
Then, with probability at least $1-\delta$ over the simulated panels, the $\alpha$-trimmed
empirical market reward $\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}$ is welfare-consistent on $\Ac_c$: its maximizer
over $\Ac_c$ lies in $\Ac^\star(x)$. Consequently any policy maximizing the robust RLDF reward
over $\Ac_c$ at $x$ places its mass on the welfare-optimal set, and a policy doing so for
$\Dc$-a.e.\ $x$ maximizes the alignment target $J^\star$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:rank} to $(a^\star,a')$ for each welfare-suboptimal $a'\in\Ac_c$.
Hypotheses (A1)--(A3) are exactly its hypotheses: (A1) supplies
$\mathrm{mean}(F_a)=\Wbar(x,a)$ on the faithful mass, (A2) supplies $\eps<\alpha$, (A3) is the
margin condition with slack $s$. The lemma gives
$\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(a^\star)>\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(a')$ with failure probability
$\le4\exp(-2ns^2(1-2\alpha)^2)$ for that pair. By (A4) this is $\le\delta/|\Ac_c|$, so a union
bound over the at most $|\Ac_c|$ suboptimal candidates makes the total failure probability
$\le\delta$. On the complement, $a^\star$ strictly outscores every other candidate, hence
$\argmax_{a\in\Ac_c}\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(a)=\{a^\star\}\subseteq\Ac^\star(x)$. The final
sentence is the pointwise decomposition following Definition~\ref{def:target}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[RLVR recovery]
\label{cor:rlvr}
In the regime of Proposition~\ref{prop:rlvr}, equivalently an outcome-verifiable task
(Definition~\ref{def:vo}) with a single consumer, one has $\eps=0$, zero sampling variance (so
any $s>0$ and $n=1$ satisfy (A4)), and welfare equal to the grade. Taking $\alpha\to0$, the
margin condition reduces to $\Wbar^\star>\Wbar(x,a')$, i.e.\ to $a^\star$ being strictly correct
and $a'$ not. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} then recovers the exact welfare-consistency of RLVR
asserted in Proposition~\ref{prop:rlvr}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
With $\eps=0$ and $\alpha\to0$ the penalty $4\alpha+\frac{2\eps}{1-2\alpha}\to0$, leaving
$M>2s$; with deterministic outcomes the empirical trimmed mean equals the grade exactly, so
any $s>0$ works and (A4) holds at $n=1$. The surviving requirement
$\Wbar^\star>\Wbar(x,a')$ is correctness of $a^\star$ over $a'$.
\end{proof}


\section{Welfare-consistency under approximate calibration}
\label{sec:approxcalib}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} assumes the simulate-then-evaluate pipeline is \emph{exactly} unbiased for
welfare on the faithful mass (Assumption~\ref{ass:calib}). Real graders are not exactly
calibrated; learned reward models are in fact systematically overconfident
\cite{rewardcalib}. We show the guarantee degrades gracefully and we pin down how much
miscalibration it tolerates.

\begin{assumption}[$\eta$-approximate calibration]
\label{ass:approxcalib}
There is $\eta\ge0$ such that, for every faithful type $u$ and every $(x,a)$,
$\bigl|\rho(x,u,a)-W(x,u,a)\bigr|\le\eta$.
\end{assumption}

Under Assumption~\ref{ass:approxcalib} the clean faithful-grade mean is within $\eta$ of welfare:
$\bigl|\mathrm{mean}(F_a)-\Wbar(x,a)\bigr|=\bigl|\E_{u\sim P_{\mathrm f}}[\rho(x,u,a)-W(x,u,a)]\bigr|\le\eta$.
Carrying this one extra bias term through the chain of Lemma~\ref{lem:rank} costs $2\eta$ in the
margin.

\begin{theorem}[Consistency under approximate calibration]
\label{thm:approxcalib}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with Assumption~\ref{ass:calib} replaced by
$\eta$-approximate calibration (Assumption~\ref{ass:approxcalib}), if for every welfare-suboptimal
$a'\in\Ac_c$
\[
  \Wbar(x,a^\star)-\Wbar(x,a')\;>\;4\alpha+\frac{2\eps}{1-2\alpha}+2s+2\eta,
\]
then with probability at least $1-\delta$ (same panel size as \textup{(A4)}) the $\alpha$-trimmed
reward $\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}$ is welfare-consistent on $\Ac_c$. The condition is sufficient; the
$2\eta$ term is necessary in the sense of Proposition~\ref{prop:calibtight} below.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Repeat the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rank} with one additional term. For the clean law $F_a$,
$|\mu_\alpha(F_a)-\Wbar(x,a)|\le|\mu_\alpha(F_a)-\mathrm{mean}(F_a)|+|\mathrm{mean}(F_a)-\Wbar(x,a)|\le2\alpha+\eta$
by Lemma~\ref{lem:three}(i) and Assumption~\ref{ass:approxcalib}. Hence, chaining the sampling and
contamination bounds of Lemma~\ref{lem:three} exactly as before,
\[
  \hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(a^\star)\ge\Wbar^\star-2\alpha-\eta-\tfrac{\eps}{1-2\alpha}-s,
  \qquad
  \hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(a')\le\Wbar(x,a')+2\alpha+\eta+\tfrac{\eps}{1-2\alpha}+s,
\]
so their difference is at least $M-4\alpha-\frac{2\eps}{1-2\alpha}-2s-2\eta>0$ under the stated
margin. The union bound over $\Ac_c$ is identical to Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Tightness of the calibration term]
\label{prop:calibtight}
The dependence on $\eta$ cannot be removed. Even with $\alpha=0$, $\eps=0$, and an infinite panel
($s=0$), there exist a query and a candidate pair $\{a^\star,a'\}$ with
$\Wbar(x,a^\star)-\Wbar(x,a')=M$ and an $\eta$-approximately-calibrated grader for which the
\textup{(exact)} reward ranks $a'$ strictly above $a^\star$ whenever $\eta\ge M/2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With $\alpha=\eps=s=0$ the reward is the exact faithful mean
$\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(a)=\mathrm{mean}(F_a)$, which Assumption~\ref{ass:approxcalib} pins only to
$[\Wbar(x,a)-\eta,\Wbar(x,a)+\eta]$. Choose a grader with
$\mathrm{mean}(F_{a^\star})=\Wbar(x,a^\star)-\eta$ and
$\mathrm{mean}(F_{a'})=\Wbar(x,a')+\eta$; both are admissible. The reward gap is then
$(\Wbar^\star-\eta)-(\Wbar(x,a')+\eta)=M-2\eta$, which is $\le0$ once $\eta\ge M/2$, so $a'$ is
weakly preferred while being welfare-inferior.
\end{proof}

\section{Regret of the training loop}
\label{sec:regret}

The results so far bound the reward at a fixed query and certify that its maximizer over a
candidate set is welfare-optimal. We now lift this to the policy an optimizer actually returns,
and isolate the part of the welfare gap that no amount of optimization removes.

Let $\Pi$ be the policy class the optimizer searches (for instance the set reachable by the
training algorithm from the base model). For $\pi\in\Pi$ write the true welfare value and the
empirical robust-reward value
\[
  V^\star(\pi)\;=\;\E_{x\sim\Dc}\,\E_{a\sim\pi(\cdot\given x)}\big[\Wbar(x,a)\big],
  \qquad
  \widehat V(\pi)\;=\;\E_{x\sim\Dc}\,\E_{a\sim\pi(\cdot\given x)}\big[\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(x,a)\big],
\]
where $\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(x,a)$ is the $\alpha$-trimmed empirical market reward at $x$. Let
$\pi^\star\in\argmax_{\pi\in\Pi}V^\star(\pi)$ be the welfare-optimal policy \emph{within the
class}. Collecting the four error sources of the preceding sections gives a uniform reward error:
on the high-probability event that every panel deviates by at most its sampling tolerance,
\[
  \bigl|\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(x,a)-\Wbar(x,a)\bigr|\;\le\;b
  \;\defeq\;\underbrace{2\alpha}_{\text{trimming}}
  +\underbrace{\tfrac{\eps}{1-2\alpha}}_{\text{contamination}}
  +\underbrace{\eta}_{\text{calibration}}
  +\underbrace{s_n}_{\text{sampling}},
  \qquad s_n=\tfrac{1}{1-2\alpha}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2n}\log\tfrac{2}{\delta'}},
\]
by Lemma~\ref{lem:three}(i)--(iii) and Assumption~\ref{ass:approxcalib}, with $\delta'$ allocated
across panels by a union bound.

\begin{theorem}[Reward error to welfare regret]
\label{thm:regret}
Suppose $\bigl|\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(x,a)-\Wbar(x,a)\bigr|\le b$ for all $(x,a)$ in the support of
the candidate sets, so that $|\widehat V(\pi)-V^\star(\pi)|\le b$ for all $\pi\in\Pi$. If $\hat\pi$
is an $\eps_{\mathrm{opt}}$-approximate maximizer of $\widehat V$ over $\Pi$, that is
$\widehat V(\hat\pi)\ge\sup_{\pi\in\Pi}\widehat V(\pi)-\eps_{\mathrm{opt}}$, then
\[
  V^\star(\hat\pi)\;\ge\;V^\star(\pi^\star)\;-\;2b\;-\;\eps_{\mathrm{opt}}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The pointwise bound gives $|\widehat V(\pi)-V^\star(\pi)|\le b$ by taking expectations over
$x\sim\Dc$ and $a\sim\pi$. Then
\[
  V^\star(\hat\pi)\;\ge\;\widehat V(\hat\pi)-b
  \;\ge\;\sup_{\pi\in\Pi}\widehat V(\pi)-\eps_{\mathrm{opt}}-b
  \;\ge\;\widehat V(\pi^\star)-\eps_{\mathrm{opt}}-b
  \;\ge\;V^\star(\pi^\star)-2b-\eps_{\mathrm{opt}}. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[KL-regularized optimizer]
\label{rem:kl}
Practical RLHF and GRPO maximize a KL-regularized objective
$J_\beta(\pi)=\widehat V(\pi)-\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})$ against a reference
policy $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$. Its maximizer $\hat\pi_\beta$ is not the $\widehat V$-maximizer, so the
clean reduction acquires one explicit regularization term. From
$J_\beta(\hat\pi_\beta)\ge J_\beta(\pi^\star)$ and $\mathrm{KL}(\hat\pi_\beta\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})\ge0$,
\[
  V^\star(\hat\pi_\beta)\;\ge\;V^\star(\pi^\star)\;-\;2b\;-\;\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi^\star\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}),
\]
so regularization costs at most $\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi^\star\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})$ and vanishes as
$\beta\to0$. The optimization term $\eps_{\mathrm{opt}}$ is what a convergence analysis of the
policy-gradient step under a biased reward oracle controls \cite{biasedpg}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Which part of the floor is irreducible]
\label{rem:floor}
As the panel grows ($s_n\to0$) and the optimizer converges ($\eps_{\mathrm{opt}}\to0$), the bound
stalls at $2\bigl(2\alpha+\tfrac{\eps}{1-2\alpha}+\eta\bigr)$. The contamination and calibration
parts of this floor are not an artifact of the analysis: Proposition~\ref{prop:calibtight}
($\eta\ge M/2$) and Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp} ($\eps\ge\alpha$) exhibit instances on which the
robust reward genuinely mis-ranks by those amounts, so a reward-driven optimizer is steered into
the corresponding welfare loss. The trimming part is a worst-case bound on the estimand shift and
is the one term that improves under grade concentration, to $O(\alpha\sigma)$, by the remark
following Lemma~\ref{lem:three}.
\end{remark}

\section{From per-query to per-distribution guarantees}
\label{sec:distributional}

Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and~\ref{thm:approxcalib} hold at a fixed query and certify consistency on
its candidate set. We now give a guarantee over the query distribution $\Dc$ and identify the
regime in which no guarantee is possible.

For a query $x$ let the welfare margin be
$M(x)=\Wbar(x,a^\star(x))-\max_{a'\notin\Ac^\star(x)}\Wbar(x,a')$, the gap between the best and the
best suboptimal candidate (set $M(x)=\infty$ if every candidate is optimal). Queries with a small
margin are the ones a sampled reward cannot resolve, so the right structural assumption is a bound
on how much $\Dc$-mass sits near zero margin.

\begin{theorem}[Per-distribution welfare regret]
\label{thm:dist}
Let $\hat a$ select $\argmax_{a\in\Ac_c(x)}\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(x,a)$ at each $x$, under the
per-query conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:approxcalib} with penalty
$p=4\alpha+\frac{2\eps}{1-2\alpha}+2s+2\eta$ and per-query failure probability at most $\delta$.
Suppose a margin-distribution bound $\Prob_{x\sim\Dc}(M(x)<\tau)\le\psi(\tau)$ holds for a
nondecreasing $\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]$. Then the $\Dc$-expected welfare regret obeys
\[
  \E_{x\sim\Dc}\bigl[\Wbar(x,a^\star(x))-\Wbar(x,\hat a(x))\bigr]\;\le\;\psi(p)+\delta.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x$. If $M(x)>p$ and the panels at $x$ succeed, an event of probability at least $1-\delta$,
then Theorem~\ref{thm:approxcalib} gives $\hat a(x)\in\Ac^\star(x)$ and the per-query regret is $0$.
Otherwise the per-query regret is at most the welfare range, which is $1$ because $W\in[0,1]$. The
bad set is contained in $\{M(x)\le p\}\cup\{\text{panel fails}\}$, of probability at most
$\psi(p)+\delta$. Taking expectations and bounding the regret by $1$ on the bad set gives the
claim.
\end{proof}

As the penalty $p\to0$ the bound tends to $\psi(0^+)+\delta$, so when the margin density has no
atom at zero and the panels are large, the robust-argmax policy is welfare-optimal for
$\Dc$-almost every query. The next proposition shows the contamination condition $\eps<\alpha$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is exactly the boundary of what any trimmed-mean reward can promise.

\begin{proposition}[Sharpness of the $\eps<\alpha$ threshold]
\label{prop:sharp}
Fix $\alpha\in(0,\tfrac12)$. For every $\eps>\alpha$ there is an $\eps$-contaminated instance, a
query with two candidates of positive welfare gap, on which the $\alpha$-trimmed empirical reward
ranks the manipulation strictly above the welfare-optimal response for \emph{every} panel size
$n$. Hence no panel size restores consistency once $\eps\ge\alpha$, and the condition $\eps<\alpha$
cannot be relaxed for the trimmed-mean aggregator.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $a^\star$ be welfare-optimal with all clean grades at a value $w^\star\in(0,1)$, so
$\hat\mu_\alpha^{(n)}(a^\star)=w^\star$ for every $n$. Let $a^\dagger$ be a manipulation whose
gameable fraction $\eps>\alpha$ places its grades at the ceiling $1$ and whose remaining
$(1-\eps)$ faithful grades sit at $0$, so its grade law has quantile function $F^{-1}(p)=0$ for
$p<1-\eps$ and $F^{-1}(p)=1$ for $p\ge1-\eps$. Its $\alpha$-trimmed functional is
\[
  \mu_\alpha(F)=\frac{1}{1-2\alpha}\int_\alpha^{1-\alpha}F^{-1}(p)\,dp=\frac{\eps-\alpha}{1-2\alpha},
\]
because the retained range $[\alpha,1-\alpha]$ still contains an $(\eps-\alpha)$ mass of ceiling
grades that trimming the top $\alpha$ does not remove. This value is independent of $n$ and exceeds
$w^\star$ whenever $w^\star<(\eps-\alpha)/(1-2\alpha)$, which is achievable since the right-hand
side is positive for $\eps>\alpha$. For any such instance the manipulation outranks the optimum at
every $n$, although $\Wbar(x,a^\star)=w^\star>\Wbar(x,a^\dagger)=(1-\eps)\cdot0+\eps\cdot w_g$ for a
manipulation with true gameable-welfare $w_g$ small. The breakdown point of the $\alpha$-trimmed
mean is therefore $\alpha$, matching the sufficient condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}


\section{Scope of the assumptions}
\label{sec:discussion}

The theorem is only as strong as its hypotheses. The one that carries the weight is calibration.

\paragraph{Calibration (A1) is load-bearing.}
Everything reduces to it. Lemma~\ref{lem:hindsight} is calibration plus the tower property, and
the robust machinery of Section~\ref{sec:robust} only protects calibration against a minority
that violates it. If the simulator is biased on the faithful majority, so that
$\E_o[g(o)\given x,u,a]$ departs systematically from $W(x,u,a)$ for typical $u$, then no trimming
level recovers welfare, because the contamination model assumes the bulk is clean. In this sense
RLDF inherits the reliability of its world model: the reward is verifier-grade only to the extent
that $g\circ T$ is calibrated and policy-independent (Definition~\ref{def:vo}). On
outcome-verifiable tasks this holds by construction ($\eps=0$); on simulated-outcome tasks it is
an empirical claim about the simulator that must be argued, not assumed. The role of
Section~\ref{sec:robust} is to weaken the requirement from ``calibrated everywhere'' to
``calibrated on a $(1-\eps)$ majority with $\eps<\alpha$,'' not to remove it.

\paragraph{Targeting is excluded by (A2), not defeated by trimming.}
Trimming caps the influence of a corrupt fraction at $\eps/(1-2\alpha)$ only when $\eps<\alpha$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:three}(ii)); once a panel is corrupt-majority ($\eps>\alpha$), the trimmed
mean's breakdown point is crossed and the bound is void. A type-aware targeting adversary
(Remark~\ref{rem:target}) is dangerous precisely because it can try to manufacture such a
corrupt-majority panel by routing the manipulation only to gameable consumers. Trimming alone
does not stop this. What stops it is the representative-panel construction of
Definition~\ref{def:market}: every candidate is graded on a fresh i.i.d.\ draw from
$P(\cdot\given x)$, so each panel's corrupt share equals the population $\eps$ no matter how the
policy routes responses, which is exactly hypothesis (A2). Thus (A2) does not quietly assume the
attack away; it is the content of a concrete design choice, representative grading, that an
implementer either makes or forfeits. Given (A2), trimming does the remaining work, and does it
without detecting which consumers are gameable; only the population-level bound on how many
matters. The irreducible assumption is global: if the faithful are a population minority
($\eps>\alpha$ everywhere), no aggregation rule recovers welfare.

\paragraph{Margin (A3) and constants.}
The margin condition demands that the welfare gap between a good response and a manipulation
exceed the summed trimming, contamination, and sampling penalties. The constants are worst-case:
the trimming term shrinks to $O(\alpha\sigma)$ under bounded grade variance, and the
contamination term is the price of admitting an $\eps$-minority one declines to detect. The
qualitative content is robust: a sufficiently distinguishable welfare difference is recoverable,
and one finer than the noise floor is not, as for any sampled reward.

\paragraph{Population diversity.}
The population kernel $P(\cdot\given x)$ is what separates RLDF from a single-judge hindsight
reward. A degenerate $P$ collapses Lemma~\ref{lem:hindsight} to a one-evaluator scheme and
forfeits both the concentration of Lemma~\ref{lem:concentration} and the robustness of
Section~\ref{sec:robust}; a diverse $P$ is what gives the trimmed mean a distribution to trim.
Heterogeneity is therefore a requirement of the construction, not a complication: it is what the
welfare average and its robust surrogate are defined over.


\section{Positioning against prior work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{RLVR and its soft extensions.}
RLVR \cite{deepseekr1,tulu3,deepseekmath} is the $\eps=0$, deterministic, single-consumer limit
(Proposition~\ref{prop:rlvr}, Corollary~\ref{cor:rlvr}). Soft extensions that learn a generative
reward model or rubric critic for open-ended domains \cite{crossingbridge,rlmt,truthrl} re-admit
a corruptible response-only grader and so live on the foresight side of
Lemma~\ref{lem:foresight}: they score the answer, not its consequence.

\paragraph{Hindsight simulation.}
RLHS \cite{rlhs} is the direct antecedent and the source, in substance, of
Lemmas~\ref{lem:foresight}--\ref{lem:hindsight}: it simulates the downstream outcome and grades
in hindsight, with automated LLM simulator and evaluator, to mitigate sycophancy and deception.
Its evaluator is a single persona. The present results add the population kernel
(Section~\ref{sec:market}), the closed-form gameability threshold (Section~\ref{sec:game}) that
ties the construction to the user-feedback manipulation result of \cite{williams}, and the
robust-aggregation ranking guarantee (Section~\ref{sec:robust}) that a single-persona scheme
cannot state.

\paragraph{User-feedback RL and its hazards.}
Training on simulated user feedback induces targeted manipulation when even a small fraction of
users is gameable \cite{williams}, which is exactly Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold} and
Remark~\ref{rem:target}, with representative panels and trimming as the countermeasure. Emergent
misalignment under competition for audiences \cite{moloch} and deceptive policies that persist
through safety training \cite{sleeper} are the qualitative hazards the calibration discussion of
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} keeps in view.

\paragraph{Robust aggregation and Byzantine-robust learning.}
The trimmed-mean reward of Section~\ref{sec:robust} imports a tool from robust statistics
\cite{lugosimendelson} and from Byzantine-robust distributed learning, where coordinate-wise
trimmed means attain optimal statistical rates while tolerating a corrupt minority below the
breakdown point \cite{yin}. The transfer is exact: a gameable subpopulation plays the role of
Byzantine workers, and the condition $\eps<\alpha$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is the breakdown point
turned into a training guarantee, with the matching converse of Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp}. What
is new is not the aggregator but the object it aggregates, the realized welfare of a consumer
population, and the coupling of the breakdown threshold to a welfare-consistency statement.

\paragraph{Decision-focused learning and social choice.}
Grading the realized outcome of acting on a response, rather than the response, is the
decision-focused-learning idea \cite{spo} carried into RLHF: the loss is the regret of the
decision a prediction induces, not the prediction error. RLDF supplies the pieces that idea needs
for a language model, a simulated decision environment and a population over which the regret is
taken. The aggregation step is also a social-choice rule over a heterogeneous population
\cite{conitzer}, and the welfare target $J^\star=\E_{u\sim P}[W]$ is its utilitarian aggregate;
population-proportional alignment \cite{popalign} matches the policy to the evaluator distribution,
whereas RLDF asks only for the weaker ranking-consistency an optimizer needs. Foresight preference
optimization is, by contrast, subject to reward-model overoptimization \cite{overopt}, the Goodhart
failure that consequence-grading is meant to sidestep.

\paragraph{Market and multi-agent framings.}
Simulated consumer populations appear in economic LLM sandboxes \cite{malles} and
generative-agent simulations \cite{genagents}, and market mechanisms have been proposed as
alignment scaffolds over agents' propositions \cite{marketmaking} and over emotional verifiable
signals \cite{rlver}. These price stated preferences or beliefs; RLDF prices realized welfare
outcomes of acting on advice, aggregated over a heterogeneous population, and feeds the aggregate
back as the reinforcement signal. We use the word market in a weak sense, a population
aggregating realized outcomes into a price, and not in the mechanism-design sense of a clearing
price, a scoring rule, or competition between responses; the object that does the work is the
robust population aggregate $\mu_\alpha$, and no result depends on the metaphor.
